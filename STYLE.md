# Upstream idea style

The reader is one person, often between gym sets, who swapped doomscrolling for this. Every card must leave them sharper than a reel would. If a card would not survive "so what?", cut it.

## One idea per card
- **title** (≤ 80 chars): the claim itself, not a teaser. "Plan the failure before it happens", not "The surprising trick experts use".
- **body** (150-600 chars): the idea in plain words, with one concrete detail (a number, a name, a scene). Readable in about 30 seconds.
- **deeper** (400-1400 chars), three short paragraphs in this order:
  1. *Why it works*: the mechanism or the evidence.
  2. *Example*: a specific case.
  3. *Apply it today*: one action small enough to do this week, starting with a verb.
  Separate the paragraphs with a blank line (`\n\n`). No headings, no bullets, no markdown: the app shows plain text.

## Truth
- Every idea names a **real source** that actually contains it: a book (title + author), a paper, an organization's guidance, or a Wikipedia article. Never invent a source, quote, study or statistic. If you are not sure a number is right, leave the number out.
- At most one direct quote per idea, under 25 words, attributed. Everything else is in your own words; never copy passages from books.
- Prefer mainstream, well-replicated findings. If a famous finding failed to replicate (ego depletion, power posing), either skip it or say so.
- **Survival & safety**: follow current mainstream guidance (Red Cross, American Heart Association, WHO, CDC, national emergency services). Start the action with calling for help where that applies. End *Apply it today* by pointing to hands-on training; reading is not training.

## Voice
- Second person, direct, warm, no hype. No "game-changer", "unlock", "secret", "hack", "mind-blowing", exclamation marks, emoji, or long dashes (use a comma, colon or full stop instead).
- Concrete beats abstract. Short sentences. Explain any jargon the first time it appears.
- Not self-help mush: every card must contain something checkable or usable.

## Topics (balance each pack evenly across all four)
- `mind`: psychology, cognitive biases, decision-making, Stoicism and other philosophy, mental models, mindset.
- `world`: history, science, economics, geopolitics, how things work.
- `life`: money, health and fitness, sleep, communication, negotiation, career, relationships.
- `survival`: first aid, emergencies, disasters, outdoors, personal and online safety.

## Tags
1-4 per idea, lowercase, hyphenated (`decision-making`, `stoicism`, `first-aid`). Reuse existing tags from `index/titles.txt` before inventing new ones.

## Journeys
A journey is a short course: 5-7 steps on one theme, in one topic, read in order. Each step is a full idea card (same fields and limits as above) that builds on the step before it, from the core idea to practice. The **title** (10-60 chars) names what you will be able to do or see by the end ("Stop serious bleeding until help arrives", "Think like a Stoic for a week"). The **summary** (40-240 chars) says who it is for and what the steps cover. Step ids are the journey id plus a 3-digit number.
